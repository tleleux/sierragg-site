Ideal customer profile · Sierra Global Groups LLC · 2026-10-09

The builder who outgrew the bookkeeper

Who the outreach targets, what they are feeling the week we reach them, where to find them in bulk, and how to score a scraped lead before anyone spends a message on it.

Owner-operator of a Florida company doing $1M to $15M, often first-generation Hispanic, bilingual or Spanish-dominant, running finance on a bookkeeper and a CPA at tax time. Profitable on paper, surprised by cash, pricing by instinct, and a lender or a large contract on the horizon.

Avatar A · primary (80% of outreach)

Snapshot

  • Role: owner, founder, president or CEO. The spouse often co-decides.
  • Company: $1M to $15M revenue, 10 to 80 employees, 5 to 20 years old, Florida (Tampa Bay, Orlando, Miami-Dade, Broward).
  • Industries: construction subcontractors and specialty trades (HVAC, roofing, electrical, concrete, flooring), food manufacturing and distribution, logistics and trucking, import-export and wholesale, medical, dental and aesthetic clinics, restaurant groups, cleaning and landscaping companies.
  • Background: first-generation from Colombia, Venezuela, Mexico, Cuba, Puerto Rico or Central America. Speaks Spanish at home and on the shop floor, English with the bank.
  • Finance stack today: QuickBooks, a part-time or family bookkeeper, a CPA once a year, the bank app as the dashboard.

What they say

"Vendemos más y hay menos plata."We sell more and there is less money. The cash-cycle complaint, verbatim.

"My accountant does my taxes. He doesn't tell me what to do."The gap between compliance and direction.

"I can't take that contract. I'd have to front payroll and materials for 60 days."Growth refused for lack of a forecast.

"The bank asked for projections. I sent them last year's P&L."The lender trigger.

Triggers worth timing outreach to

  • An SBA, equipment or commercial loan application (public in SBA 7(a) and 504 data after approval).
  • A new large contract or a second location announced on LinkedIn or Facebook.
  • A new contractor license, a new entity on Sunbiz, or a name change (reorganization).
  • Revenue crossing $1M, $5M or $10M, visible through headcount jumps and hiring posts.
  • Succession talk: a son or daughter named as a manager, a "next generation" post.
  • Tax season (February to April) and the quarterly estimated-tax dates.

Buying psychology

  • Wants an operator who has run a company, not a consultant with a framework. Proof of having been a CFO beats certifications.
  • Trust forms in Spanish and through shared context: how a family business actually pays people, how suppliers in Latin America negotiate, what a bank in Florida asks for.
  • Needs value before paying. The free forecast walkthrough is not a gimmick to them; it is the test.
  • Retainer-averse until a deliverable has landed. Enter through the Express Assessment, upgrade to the monthly engagement after the first report.
  • Decision cycle 2 to 6 weeks. The owner decides; the spouse and the CPA are consulted. Bring the CPA in as an ally, never as a competitor.

Disqualifiers

  • Under about $750K revenue: cannot sustain a CFO engagement. Offer the hourly session or Foundation tier only.
  • No bookkeeping at all: route to the Foundation tier first, then revisit.
  • Already has a controller, VP Finance or CFO on LinkedIn.
  • Pure cash retail with no receivables and no inventory: less cash-cycle pain, lower fit (pricing pain may still apply).
  • Companies in active litigation, insolvency or tax enforcement: refer out.

Avatar B · secondary (20% of outreach)

The Latin American company landing in Florida

  • Who: owner or CEO of a Colombian, Mexican, Peruvian, Ecuadorian or Central American company doing $2M to $50M at home, wanting a U.S. entity to sell, distribute or open an office.
  • Pain: does not know the sequence (entity, EIN, bank, compliance calendar, operating budget in dollars) and has been quoted by lawyers for pieces, not the whole path.
  • Proof that lands: Natalia built Smart Translators LLC in Tampa for a Colombian company in 2020, from business plan to operations.
  • Where: ProColombia and ProMéxico-type agencies, Colombian-American and other binational chambers in Florida, trade shows in Miami (food and beverage, logistics, construction), LinkedIn companies headquartered in Latin America whose posts mention Florida, Miami, Tampa or "USA".
  • Language: Spanish first, always.
