A CFO who has run the company, for owners who built one without one

What Natalia Sierra sells, the mechanism that makes it different, the moat, the offer ladder, and the terms of the TTG middleman deal. For Ty's understanding and for Natalia to react to.

Positioning: Sierra Global Groups is the bilingual fractional CFO for owner-led Florida companies between $1M and $15M. Natalia spent ten years as the CFO inside manufacturing and services companies, then built a U.S. subsidiary herself. She brings that operating finance to companies that have outgrown the bookkeeper but cannot justify a full-time CFO.

The core offer

External Strategic Management (her name for the fractional CFO retainer). Ongoing financial direction for the owner and the management team: a 13-week cash forecast maintained weekly, a short indicator set, a monthly executive meeting, an action plan with owners and dates, and preparation of whatever a bank, partner or buyer asks to see. Delivered in English or Spanish, remote, with her bookkeeper and CPA kept in the loop rather than replaced.

The unique mechanism: the Cost-to-Cash System

Most advisors start at the forecast. Natalia starts at the cost system, because a forecast built on uncosted prices is fiction. The chain she installs:

LinkWhat gets builtOwner's question it answers
1 · Cost systemTrue cost per product, job or client, overhead allocatedWhich work actually makes money?
2 · Price and marginPrices from margin targets; a margin floor per lineWhat should we charge, and to whom?
3 · Cash cycleReceivable terms, supplier terms, inventory days, timed togetherWhy does growth drain the bank?
4 · Decisions13-week cash forecast, indicator set, monthly executive meetingCan we afford this hire, this machine, this contract?

Why it is credible from her: a Master's thesis on cost systems for industrial companies, five years as CFO of a food manufacturer, and the operating plan that tripled installed capacity at Smart Translators. The mechanism is the thing she has done repeatedly, named so an owner can remember it.

The moat

Three things that are each common and together rare

  • Operator, not advisor. Ten years owning finance, administration and HR at once inside real companies. She has approved payroll on a bad cash week.
  • Bilingual at CFO level. Florida has thousands of Spanish-speaking bookkeepers and tax preparers and very few people who can run a CFO conversation in Spanish.
  • Has done the U.S. landing herself. Built Smart Translators LLC in Tampa in 2020: plan, projections, entity, banking, compliance, operations.

Who she competes with, and why they miss this buyer

  • Local CPA firms: compliance and tax; no forecasting, no cost systems, no monthly direction.
  • National fractional CFO firms: English-only, built for SaaS and venture-backed companies, uncomfortable with a family trucking company's books.
  • Online bookkeeping-plus-CFO platforms: dashboards without an operator; no one who will sit with the owner and the spouse.
  • Spanish-speaking "contadores": trusted, but tax-focused; they are allies to bring in, not rivals.

Value equation, in her offer

  • Dream outcome: never surprised by cash again; prices that make money; a bank-ready company.
  • Likelihood: their own numbers, a named mechanism, an operator's track record, a documented report after every engagement.
  • Time delay: value in 60 seconds (tool), a real forecast in one call, a report in three sessions.
  • Effort: she works with the bookkeeper and CPA they already have; the owner sends statements and shows up to one meeting a month.
  • Guarantee to consider (her decision): Express Assessment, "if the report does not show you at least one decision worth more than the fee, keep the report and pay nothing." Performance-flavored, fits an operator's confidence.

The TTG deal: middleman on leads, revenue share on closed engagements

TTG Management (Ty) providesSierra Global Groups (Natalia) provides
List building (Sales Navigator, Sunbiz, DBPR, SBA data, chambers), scoring, LinkedIn and email outreach (Closely, Apollo), the website and tool, a GHL sub-account with chatbot and report automation, booking, nurture, and the nightly pulse on booked calls and proposalsDelivery of the free walkthroughs within her weekly cap, the sales conversation, proposals, pricing, engagement delivery, and the proof points (case studies) that feed the next round of outreach
  • Structure to agree: revenue share on closed engagements sourced by TTG, paid monthly as she collects, for a defined period (12 months is the common frame) plus a small setup fee for the build. Percentage is a decision for the two of you; it should reflect that TTG carries the acquisition cost and she carries delivery.
  • Attribution rule: every lead lands in a TTG-owned GHL sub-account before anything else. A monthly reconciliation lists sourced leads, walkthroughs, proposals and closed retainers. No arguable attribution.
  • Capacity rule: she states how many free walkthroughs a week she can deliver; the chatbot offers the tool first once the week is full.
  • Data rule: bank statements go to her, never into TTG systems beyond the booking record.
  • Exit rule: either side can stop new sourcing with 30 days' notice; revenue share continues on engagements already closed for the agreed period.
